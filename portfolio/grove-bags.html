Overview

The science first.
The sell follows.

Grove Bags didn't need convincing that their product works, growers, extractors, and dispensaries already knew that. What they needed was a store that could carry a genuinely technical product line, TerpLoc® barrier film, auto-curing pouches, SafeVac rolls, without drowning customers in jargon or slowing down the checkout for someone who just wants a case of ounce pouches by Friday.

We rebuilt the site around three very different buyers: the home grower ordering a single sleeve, the processor ordering pallets, and the dispensary chasing a custom branded run. Same catalog, three completely different paths through it. The copy leans into what actually matters to a cultivator, weight retention, terpene loss, shelf stability, instead of generic e-commerce filler.

Process

How it came together

Grove Bags sits at an odd intersection, industrial packaging science sold through a consumer storefront. Getting that balance right took three phases, none of which we rushed.

01
Discovery

Before touching layout, we sat with the Grove Bags team to understand TerpLoc® itself, how the film actually manages moisture and oxygen, and why a cultivator cares about that at 2am during a harvest. That vocabulary became the backbone of every product page.

02
Design

We moved the palette away from generic dispensary green-on-white and toward something closer to the product itself, dark, earthy, a little industrial. Photography of the actual bag texture and zipper detail replaced stock cannabis imagery throughout.

03
Development

Built on Shopify with a custom theme to handle a genuinely large catalog, single pouches, bulk cases, and made-to-order custom runs, without the site feeling cluttered. Filtering by user type (cultivator, distributor, dispensary) was baked into navigation from day one.

Design: Mobile & Details

Results

The outcome

Post-launch, Grove Bags saw a real shift in how the catalog performed, fewer abandoned carts on the bulk-case pages, more time spent on the technology explainer, and a checkout that no longer felt like an afterthought bolted onto a science project.
